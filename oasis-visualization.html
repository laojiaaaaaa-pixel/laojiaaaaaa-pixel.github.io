Research motivation

The same news can produce different judgments across users.

Users may rely on source reputation, AI disclosure, content quality, or social signals when evaluating the same article. The study tests how these cues interact with user characteristics and repeated exposure.

The project examines how production cues, user characteristics, and repeated exposure shape those differences.

Inside the model · 2026—present

Simulating news exposure and trust updating with heterogeneous agents.

OASIS provides a controlled information environment for more than 1,000 heterogeneous agents. The simulation records how they evaluate and respond to news across experimental conditions and time steps.

Study population

Real news and user profiles become the shared environment for 1,000+ heterogeneous agents.

Experimental factors

AI labels, corrective information, and user types vary across experimental conditions.

Outcomes

Trust trajectories, behavioral logs, and structured reflections make individual decisions observable.

False-news experiment · Paired comparison

An AI disclosure label lowered belief before correction arrived.

The same 25 false-news items were shown with and without an AI-generated label. Across exposure, peer discussion, and expert correction, labeled agents reported less belief and more authenticity suspicion.

What I am exploring

Questions that guide my current work.

01 · T0

Production-mode effects

Hold the article constant and vary four human–AI production modes to isolate the first impression of authorship.

02 · 2×2

Production mode × content quality

Separate production identity from content quality by crossing accurate news with a single verifiable false fact.

03 · T1—T2₄

Repeated exposure

Follow matched agents across repeated exposure as familiarity, verification, and trust update over time.

04 · ABM

Information ecology

Scale individual judgment into an information ecology shaped by interaction, recommendation, correction, and heterogeneous users.
