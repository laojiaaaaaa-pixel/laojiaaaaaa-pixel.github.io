AI-Generated News and Social Media Trust

Overview

Research question and design

This project uses OASIS to place heterogeneous simulated agents in controlled social-media news environments. It isolates how the disclosed division of labor between humans and AI changes initial judgments, intended actions, and later reasoning.

Updated experiment · Illusory truth

Repeated exposure reliably increased belief in a single false claim.

Agents encountered two false-news items twice and then rated those repeated claims alongside three novel false claims. Independent cohorts were tested after 1, 2, or 7 days across all four production modes.

Interpretation

What the simulations suggest

Repetition remains powerful

Two exposures produced a stable positive illusory truth effect in every production mode and delay.

AI prompts broad skepticism

AI-dominant production lowered belief in repeated and novel false claims rather than selectively suppressing repetition.

Mechanism is not mediation

Machine heuristics and verification changed as predicted, but did not explain meaningful variation in ITE.

Current conclusion. Repetition significantly raises the credibility of a false claim. AI-dominant production activates skepticism and verification awareness and lowers overall false-claim credibility, but the relative repetition gain remains statistically unchanged.
