Research Proposal

Value Tendencies in Large Language Models and Their Social Consequences

01 · Research Motivation

AI as an intermediary of information

Large language models are rapidly becoming an important channel through which people obtain information. Increasingly, instead of comparing multiple news sources or searching for information themselves, people simply ask AI systems to explain events, summarize controversies, or provide recommendations.

As AI becomes an intermediary between humans and information, I am interested in a question that extends beyond model performance and factual accuracy.

02 · Central Question
How might the value tendencies embedded in different large language models influence the way people understand the world?

Existing studies have shown that different LLMs may exhibit different value tendencies. I believe the more consequential question is what happens after these differences reach millions of users and become part of their everyday information environments.

03 · Intellectual Foundation

From International Relations to AI

This question did not originate from my AI research, but from my training in International Relations. During my studies, I was often struck by the fact that countries could reach completely different policy conclusions while looking at the same facts.

Whether discussing territorial disputes, climate governance, or emerging technologies, disagreements rarely resulted from insufficient information. Instead, they reflected different normative assumptions and value priorities that shaped how information was interpreted. If people increasingly rely on LLMs to interpret complex social and political issues, then AI is no longer merely retrieving information—it is also participating in its interpretation.

04 · Current Research

Human–AI collaboration in information ecosystems

I am developing an agent-based simulation of online information ecosystems to study how different forms of human–AI collaboration influence trust, information diffusion, and collective opinion formation.

While building this system, I gradually realized that measuring differences between models is only the first step. What ultimately matters is whether these differences accumulate through repeated human–AI interactions and eventually reshape information ecosystems.

05 · Proposed Approach

Connecting models, behaviour, and collective outcomes

Answering this question requires more than evaluating language models. It requires connecting natural language processing, human behaviour, and computational social science. I hope to combine three complementary approaches:

LLM analysisIdentify and compare value tendencies expressed by different models.
Behavioural experimentsExamine how people interpret and respond to AI-mediated information.
Agent-based modellingStudy how repeated individual interactions may develop into collective social outcomes.

Rather than viewing AI as an isolated technical system, I hope to understand it as a social actor whose embedded value tendencies may increasingly shape public opinion and the information environments in which people make decisions.

06 · Research Preparation

An interdisciplinary perspective

My background in International Relations enables me to identify socially meaningful questions, while my experience in NLP, large-scale text analysis, and agent-based modelling allows me to investigate these questions using computational methods.

I hope to contribute this interdisciplinary perspective to research in social computing and to connect advances in AI with the broader societal transformations they may bring.
